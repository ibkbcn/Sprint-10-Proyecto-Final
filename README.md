# Fresh Products Sales in Mercamadrid: Origin Analysis and Sustainability Impact

## CONTEXT
Mercamadrid is the largest fresh food distribution hub in Spain and the largest of its kind in Europe, serving as a key reference point for understanding the structure of the fresh produce market. Its activity reflects the interactions between domestic production, imports, and internal demand, making it a barometer of the balance between food self-sufficiency and external dependency.

In this context, analyzing the origin of traded products is particularly relevant given the growing internationalization of food trade and ongoing debates around the sustainability of supply chains.

This project uses 2024 data to provide an analytical overview of the geographical origin of fresh products sold at Mercamadrid, identifying origin patterns, differences in transport distances, and their relationship with seasonality, contributing to the broader discussion on the sustainability and resilience of Spain's food system.

> **Note:** The analysis focuses on vegetable products as they offer the most balanced mix of domestic and imported supply among all categories, making them the most suitable for studying origin patterns and seasonal dynamics. While the entire vegetable sector was examined to provide a comprehensive context, the study specifically narrows its focus to the 14 top-selling product categories. These categories account for over half of total vegetable kilos sold at Mercamadrid in 2024, ensuring a representative analysis of the broader market.

---

## KEY OBJECTIVES AND QUESTIONS

* **Import Weight:** What is the real share of imports within the total supply of fresh vegetable products at Mercamadrid?
* **Seasonality Influence:** To what extent does seasonality affect the origin of vegetable products?
* **Logistics and Distance:** How far do vegetable products travel from their place of origin to Mercamadrid? Is there a significant difference between domestic and imported products?

---

## MAIN CONCLUSIONS

1. **Complementary Dependency:** Domestic supply is the backbone of food provision, but imports are essential to meet constant demand throughout the year.
2. **Consolidated Out-of-Season Supply:** The normalization of year-round product availability is a key driver of import dependency, ensuring a continuous supply of fresh produce regardless of season.
3. **High Environmental Impact:** Transport associated with imports generates a significant environmental footprint, imported products travel on average 11 times the distance of domestic ones.
